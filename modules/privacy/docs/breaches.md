# Personal data breaches

Use this guide to record a personal data breach, assess it and record who you
told. You need the `privacy.breaches.manage` permission. Under UK GDPR Article
33 you must tell the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach, unless
it is unlikely to risk people. Article 34 covers telling the people affected.
Confirm what applies to you with your own data protection adviser.

The tool records and counts the time. It sends nothing to the ICO or to
anyone else. For what to do about the incident itself, follow your incident
runbook.

## Record a breach

1. Open **Data Protection**, then **Breaches**.
2. Choose **Record breach**. The dialog says "Nothing is sent to the ICO or to
   anyone else. This only records what happened."
3. Enter **When you became aware**. This starts the 72-hour clock. It is the
   time you were aware, which may be after the breach was found.
4. Enter **When the breach was found**, and **What happened**.
5. Choose the kinds of data involved. The special category choices say so.
6. Enter roughly how many people and records, or leave them blank if you do not
   know. Describe the likely consequences and what you have done about it.
7. Choose **Record breach**.

Times in the future are refused. Do not put names in the description unless you
must. The description is treated as special category text.

## Watch the clock

The breach page shows a **72-hour clock**: "23 hours left to tell the ICO, by
3:15pm on 9 December 2026". Hours are elapsed hours, not working hours. A
weekend or a clock change does not move the deadline. Times show in your
funder's time zone.

If the deadline passes, the page says "ICO deadline passed 4 hours ago". The
breaches list shows the same.

## Assess it

Choose **Add assessment**. Say whether the breach is unlikely to risk people, a
risk to people or a high risk to people. Say whether the ICO needs to be told,
whether the people affected need to be told, and why.

Each assessment is kept as it is. Add another if the picture changes. The tool
does not decide for you. As a guide, Articles 33 and 34 set the tests: tell the
ICO unless it is unlikely to risk people, and tell the people affected when it
is a high risk. Ask your adviser if you are unsure.

## Record that you told the ICO

After you have told the ICO yourself, choose **Record that we told the ICO**.
Enter when you told them and the **ICO reference** they gave you.

If that was more than 72 hours after you became aware, you must also give a
reason: Still investigating, A processor told us late, Reported in phases, or
Another reason. If it was within 72 hours, leave the reason blank. The tool
refuses a time before you became aware.

## Record that you told the people affected

After you have told them, choose **Record that we told the people affected**.
Enter when and how: email, post, phone or in person.

## What is recorded

Recording, changing, assessing and telling each write an audit event, as does
opening a breach. The event holds ids and codes, never the description.
