# Rights requests and deadlines

Use this guide when a person asks to see, correct, restrict, move or erase
what you hold about them, or to object, or to complain. You need the
`privacy.requests.manage` permission. Under UK GDPR Article 12(3) you normally
have one month to answer. Confirm the details with your own data protection
adviser.

## Log a request

1. Open **Data Protection**, then **Requests**.
2. Choose **Record request**. The dialog says "Starts the clock from the day it
   arrived."
3. Enter **Person's record ID**. Copy it from the person's record. The server
   checks that the person exists.
4. Enter **Received**: when the request arrived, which may be before today. A
   time in the future is refused.
5. Choose the kind, how it arrived and where the identity check stands, and
   write a short summary of what the person asked for.
6. Choose **Record request**.

The kinds are access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability,
objection, automated decision and complaint. A request can arrive by email,
post, phone, in person, web form, social media or another way.

Do not paste identity documents into the summary. The request records only
where the identity check stands: Not started, Proof asked for, Verified, Not
needed or Failed. Keep the evidence in your own secure system.

To change the kind, how it arrived, the summary or the identity check, open the
request and choose to edit it, then **Save request**. The person and the
received time cannot change. If either is wrong, log a new request.

## Read the list

The **Data protection requests** list shows the soonest due date first. Stopped
clocks come last. Filter by kind, status or due within the next 7, 14, 30 or 90
days. Every status is also written as text, not only shown by colour.

## Read the clock

Each request has a **Clock** panel. The status is one of:

- **Running**: "Due 12 January 2027 (9 days left)".
- **Extended**: "Extended to 12 March 2027".
- **Clock stopped**: the due date is not known until it restarts.
- **Overdue**: "Overdue by 3 days".
- **Closed**: "Closed on 12 January 2027: completed".

Dates and times show in your funder's time zone, as set in Settings. For a
funder in the UK that is UK time.

The due date is one calendar month after the day the request arrived. If the
next month has no matching day, it is the last day of that month. If the due
date falls on a weekend or a non-working date, it moves to the next working day,
as ICO guidance says. This rule, and the extension and stop-the-clock rules
below, rest on general knowledge until the funder's solicitor confirms them.

## Stop the clock

Choose **Stop the clock** while you wait for proof of identity or for the
person to clarify the request. Enter the day it happened and why it stops.

While the clock is stopped, the due date is not known. When you choose
**Restart the clock**, the days it was stopped are added to the due date. The
rule comes from the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Check it with your adviser
before you rely on it.

You cannot stop the clock after the due date has passed. The tool says "The
deadline has passed, so the clock cannot be stopped. Respond now."

## Extend the deadline

Choose **Extend deadline** when the request is complex or the person made
several requests. Extend by 1 or 2 months. A request can be extended once, and
only on or before its due date. The tool refuses a second extension: "This
request has been extended once already, which is the limit."

The tool records the extension. It does not tell the person. You do that
yourself.

## Add tasks

On the request, use **Tasks** and **Add task**. A task has a title, a person it
is assigned to, an optional tool it concerns and a due date. Choose **Mark
done** beside a task when it is finished. A request can have up to 100 tasks.

## Close a request

Choose **Close request**, then say how it ended: Completed, Refused, Withdrawn
by the person, or Identity not confirmed. A closed request cannot change.

## What is recorded

Every change to the clock, and every time someone opens a request, writes an
audit event. The event holds ids and codes only, never the summary. The
request, its dates, clock changes and outcome stay as your record, even after
the person's data is erased.
